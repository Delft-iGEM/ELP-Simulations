READ THIS FIRST
- Two pieces of work are described: (A) SubM4, a parameter sweep of an ELP brush with free lysines, and (B) a new
  submodule on RGD+ lysine PLACEMENT (focused / clustered / random / uniform arrangements).
- Both are simulation studies of polymer brushes (CALVADOS2 coarse-grained model, one bead per residue, implicit
  solvent, OpenMM). Authors/owners: Chris (the user, runs most simulations) and Titus (teammate, runs a second set of SubM4 runs).
- Everything is derived from raw simulation output that was only READ, never modified.
- Results are PRELIMINARY where stated. Where something was a post-hoc choice it says so; keep that wording in the wiki.

======================================================================================
PART A. SubM4: what governs inter-chain crosslinking OPPORTUNITY in a lysine-containing ELP brush
======================================================================================

A1. System and design
- 16 chains of (VPGIG)n carrying 4 lysines (VPGKG), 4x4 lattice, "preattached" mode: 30 % of lysines (19 of 64) are
  tethered to the surface at t = 0 and stay there. 293.15 K, 400 ns (40 M steps x 0.01 ps), 5714 frames, one frame per 70 ps.
- Crosslinking is OFF in SubM4 runs. The response is a PROXY: the mean number of inter-chain lysine-lysine contacts per
  frame ("crosslinking opportunity" / stiffness proxy).
- Four design parameters, each coded to -1..+1 over the protocol range: grafting density C (0.01-0.06 chains/nm2),
  chain length L (50-150 pentarepeats), lysine spacing S (5-25 % of chain length, four equally spaced lysines), ionic
  strength I (0.05-0.50 M). Base case: C 0.04, L 72, S ~20.8 %, I 0.20 M.
- Run batches: anchors (replicated 3x), extra2/3/4/5 (single runs, space-filling design), Titus's extras, Titus's
  anchors A10-A19.

A2. Frozen definitions (file: analysis/settings.yaml; fixed BEFORE responses were summarised, confirmed by Chris)
- Primary response inter_free_12A: inter-chain K-K contacts per frame, lysine CA beads within 12 A, minimum-image in
  x, y, z, counting FREE lysines only (neither lysine surface-pinned). Intra-chain pairs closer than 4 residues dropped.
- Burn-in 100 ns (1428 frames); production 300 ns (4286 frames). Block-average SE (10 blocks). Drift flag |z|>3,
  Cook's D > 4/n. Random seed 20261015.
- Sensitivity responses listed in advance: 8 A, 16 A cutoffs, "team definition A" (drop only both-pinned pairs, the
  convention of earlier submodules, used in analyze.ipynb cell 20), 200 ns burn-in.
- Model: OLS on log(response), full quadratic in the 4 coded parameters (4 linear + 4 squared + 6 interactions).

A3. Data actually available (analysis/run_inventory.csv)
- 104 runs with trajectories, all QC-clean (completed, 5714 frames, single job). 84 distinct design points, 10 anchors in triplicate.
- Titus's 20 "sub4-extra" runs (non-reactive, same template): only per-run summary text files exist in the repo; the
  trajectories are on Titus's /scratch (not readable). So they enter ONLY the team-definition-A fit (124 runs), not the main analysis.
- Titus's 30 anchor runs A10-A19: crosslinking was ON (against the protocol), so they cannot join the contact fits (see A6).
- Not yet run / not yet finished: the 100 planned "sub4-main" runs (30 prepared in Chr_sub4_run5.csv, not started),
  30 non-reactive reruns of A10-A19 ("-nr", prepared, not started), sub4-extra5 rows 15-20.

A4. Main results (frozen linear-coded model, n = 104; files analysis/stats/, figures in analysis/figures/)
- R2 0.960, adjusted 0.954; grouped 5-fold CV R2 0.882 (RMSE 0.247 log units, ~28 %). Random forest CV R2 0.871.
- Share of explained variance (LMG): grafting density ~82 %, lysine spacing ~7 %, chain length ~6 %, ionic strength ~6 %.
- One-at-a-time effects from the base case (high/low across the range): density x5.4, chain length x0.46, spacing x0.62,
  ionic strength x1.27 (salt screens K+/K+ repulsion). Spacing and length cost more at high density (interactions
  C x S -0.47, C x L -0.22, L x S -0.24); nothing interacts with ionic strength.
- Noise: replicate CV 1-12 % (pooled SD of log y 0.067). Noise is multiplicative, so the log transform is right. Use
  the replicate pure error, not the per-run block SE (replicate SD is 0.3-4.4x the block SE).
- LACK OF FIT IS SIGNIFICANT: F(69,20) = 6.6, p = 1e-5 (residual SD 0.156 vs pure error 0.067). The quadratic misses the
  low-density corner and gives a spurious maximum near C ~ 0.052; the raw data rise monotonically and flatten.
  Heteroscedastic and heavy-tailed residuals; one influential run (sub4-extra2-l58-s4-c0p01-i0p43-05, Cook's D 0.95,
  low density + narrowest spacing), QC-clean and kept.
- Conclusions are robust to cutoff (8/16 A), pair definition, and burn-in (R2 0.94-0.96, same signs, p<0.02).
- Secondary metrics: Rg, end-to-end distance and brush height are governed by chain length (76-91 %). Loops (intra-chain
  contacts) are governed by spacing (54 %) and length (40 %), not density. Bridge-to-loop ratio splits density 51 %,
  spacing 25 %, length 20 %.

A5. Log-coded refit (analysis/export/models/, figures_logcoded/)  -- STATE THE POST-HOC STATUS PLAINLY
- Same quadratic, but with ln of all four parameters, each coded -1..+1 over its log range. CHOSEN AFTER the frozen
  model's lack of fit was seen; not part of the frozen plan. The frozen linear-coded model stays the baseline.
- Primary response: R2 0.960 -> 0.984, CV R2 0.882 -> 0.944, lack of fit F 6.61 -> 2.51 (p 1e-5 -> 0.012, still
  significant; without the one influential run, p = 0.09). Density effect x8.3 [7.2, 9.5] over 0.01-0.06 (vs x5.4),
  rising monotonically, no spurious maximum. Length x0.40, spacing x0.63, salt x1.25.
- Also fitted (both codings, same diagnostics): log intra-chain contacts, and the inter-chain FRACTION inter/(inter+intra)
  as a quasi-binomial GLM (logit link, pair-frame counts, estimated dispersion because frames are autocorrelated).
  Intra: spacing 54 %, length 41 %. Inter fraction (log coding): density 52 %, spacing 27 %, length 19 %, salt ~2 % (n.s.);
  longer chains RAISE the fraction (odds x3.2 across the range) although they lower the inter-chain count.
  Full table: analysis/export/models/comparison.csv.

A6. Titus's runs
- Held-out test: the team-A model fitted on our runs alone predicts Titus's 20 non-reactive extras with R2 0.83,
  RMSE 0.41 log units (our own CV 0.25), no systematic offset (p 0.44); 18/20 inside the 95 % prediction interval.
  The two misses are the two known weak spots: extra-04 (C 0.011, spacing 4.9 %, observed 0.009 vs predicted 0.048 --
  the lowest of all 124 runs) and extra-15 (C 0.060, wide spacing, observed 0.63 vs 0.37 -- no high-density turnover).
  Adding them raises the team-A lack of fit (F 7.1 -> 12.1). Log density fixes extra-15 but not extra-04.
- Reactive anchors A10-A19 (30 runs, 10 design points x 3; crosslinking 0.8 nm, p = 1, valence 1; data = Titus's table
  figures/sub4/crosslinks-submodule4-anchor.csv plus the per-run contact summaries). Contact counts are 26-95x the
  non-reactive prediction, because bonded pairs are held together; they are NOT usable as replicates.
  They were analysed on their own (analysis/scripts/06_reactive.py, stats/reactive_*):
    * Conversion is saturated: 20-22 of 22 possible bonds (45 free lysines/2) form in every run.
    * What varies is the BRIDGE FRACTION (inter-chain bonds / all bonds), 0.30-1.00. Quasi-binomial GLM, main effects
      only (10 points): spacing odds x2.6 [1.7,4.1] p<1e-4; density x1.9 [1.1,3.3] p=0.02; length x2.2 (p=0.09);
      salt x1.5 (n.s.). Spacing has the OPPOSITE sign to the non-reactive contact count: wider spacing removes loops faster.
    * Proxy check: at the 10 design points the non-reactive model's predicted bridge SHARE (inter/(inter+intra)) tracks the
      reactive bridge fraction (Spearman 0.90, Pearson 0.97, even in absolute value) while the raw inter-chain contact
      count does not (Spearman 0.32, p 0.37). Suggests promoting the bridge share to a co-primary response. THIS IS AN
      OPEN DECISION FOR CHRIS/THE TEAM, not settled. Caveats: 10 points, Titus's crosslink table was not re-derived,
      model predictions rather than paired runs.

======================================================================================
PART B. RGD+ lysine placement: which arrangement gives the most INTER-chain crosslinks, and how to optimise it
======================================================================================
(Everything in B is in analysis_lysplace/. Full write-up: analysis_lysplace/README.md.)

B1. The question and why SubM4's proxy does not apply
- Goal (from Chris): find which lysine arrangements (focused, uniform, clustered, random) give the highest number of
  inter-chain crosslinks, and define a way to call one "optimal". Crosslinking is ON in these runs, so the SubM4 contact
  proxy is not used; the response is the actual crosslink network, plus z-height structure of the brush and bonds.

B2. Data
- 130 runs from five sheets (Chr_RGD+_Lysine_Sweep.csv, Chr_RGD+_Lysine_sweep_20.csv, Chr_RGD+_Lysine_sweep_30.csv,
  Chr_RGD+_lysRandom.csv, Chr_RGD+_lysine_scramble_sweep_20.csv). Classes: clustered 17, focused 25, random 43,
  uniform 25, "scramble" 20 (the sheet without a class suffix: one lysine per block, like uniform).
- Construct (read from each run's simulated sequence, never from the name alone): 357 residues = LQ + 4 x [RGD domain
  LDASTVYAVTGRGDSPASSAASA + ELP block of (VPGXG)n + VP], blocks of 14, 11, 15, 11 repeats, X = I except 4 K (the design)
  and M at repeat 6 of blocks 2 and 4. Run names encode K repeat positions per block ("13R1G13D4" = block1 repeat 13,
  block2 repeat 1, block3 repeat 13, block4 repeat 4; "0R0G0D3+4+5+7" = four K in block 4). Checked name vs sequence for all 130.
- Common settings: 16 chains, C = 0.04 chains/nm2, 0.19 M, 293.15 K, preattached 30 % (19 of 64 K pinned, none during
  the run), crosslinking 0.8 nm, p = 1, valence 1 (so at most floor(45/2) = 22 bonds), starts at 10 ns, 400 ns.
  All 130 runs passed QC. NO DESIGN WAS SIMULATED TWICE (no replicates); noise is judged from the spread within a class.
- Class vs spacing: clustered = 4 K adjacent in one block (closest K-K spacing 5 residues); focused = 4 K in one block,
  spread (5-15); random = mixed, spacing 5-80; uniform/scramble = one K per block (30-105). Class and spacing are
  nearly the same variable; only "random" covers the whole range.

B3. Pipeline (all scripts in analysis_lysplace/scripts/, fixed seed 20261016, rerun with run_all.sh)
- 00_inventory.py: design table, K positions, design descriptors, QC.
- 01_extract.py (one pass over each DCD, ~12 s/run; extract.sbatch for the cluster): brush h50/h95 (mass-weighted),
  per-frame heights of every crosslink (midpoint z of the two bonded beads), per-lysine height, RGD (R,G,D beads of the
  four GRGDS motifs) height, Rg/Ree, 2 A z-profiles. Heights are above the wall (z_wall = 1.9 nm); z is the surface normal.
- 02_metrics.py: per-run network metrics from crosslink_events.csv (bridges = inter-chain bonds, loops = intra-chain,
  distinct chain pairs bridged, largest connected cluster, cycle rank, unreacted lysines, state at 100 ns, bond timing),
  per-lysine and per-bond tables. Consistency asserted (lysine states reproduce the network counts; 19 pinned per run).
- 03_compare.py: Kruskal-Wallis over five classes, pairwise Mann-Whitney with Holm, Cliff's delta with bootstrap CIs,
  descriptor GLM, and the frozen optimality rule.
- 04_lysine_model.py: logistic GEE on every free lysine (5850) for P(bridge) and P(loop); run-level 10-fold CV by run;
  exhaustive design search over all 211,876 placements of 4 K on the 49 non-M VPGXG positions.
- 05_figures.py: figures (Plotly, MOSAIC style; class colours are one blue ramp validated for contrast and colour-blindness,
  with marker shapes as a second cue).
- Frozen definitions: analysis_lysplace/settings.yaml, written before any network/height/timing/per-lysine quantity was
  computed. HONEST DISCLOSURE: the per-class mean bridge counts had been seen once (a data check of the event logs)
  before freezing, so the class RANKING on the primary response is not a blind result; everything else is. A dated note
  at the end of the file records what happened to the optimality rule.
- Primary response: number of inter-chain crosslinks ("bridges") at end of run.

B4. Results (medians per class: clustered / focused / random / uniform / scramble)
- Bridges 3 / 7 / 14 / 19 / 19.5 (Kruskal-Wallis p 1e-20). Loops 16 / 14 / 8 / 3 / 2.5. Crosslinks formed (of 22):
  19 / 21 / 22 / 22 / 22 -- crosslinking saturates except in clustered designs, where ~7 lysines stay unreacted.
- Every pair of classes differs on bridges (Holm p < 1e-4, Cliff's delta -0.75 to -1.0) EXCEPT uniform vs scramble
  (p 0.94, delta 0.01 [-0.32, 0.35]). Uniform and scramble differ on none of 23 responses (all Holm p > 0.6): treat
  scramble as a second uniform set (report both, pool as "one per block" where useful; 48 runs, median 19 bridges).
- Network connectivity: distinct chain pairs bridged 3 / 7 / 13 / 18 / 18; fraction of runs where all 16 chains form ONE
  connected network: 0 / 0 / 14 % / 48 % / 45 %; cycle rank 0 / 0 / 2 / 5 / 5. Even the best arrangement is fully connected
  in under half of the runs (surface not counted as a node).
- Z-STRUCTURE (the "distribution in Z height" question): in clustered and focused designs ALL crosslinks, bridges and
  loops, sit within ~3 nm of the wall (median bridge height 1.5 / 2.2 nm; 0 % of bridge-frames above the brush's half-mass
  height h50) because the pinned lysine pulls its whole K block to the surface. In random / uniform / scramble bridges sit at
  6.1 / 7.2 / 7.3 nm with ~55 / 70 / 70 % above h50, above the mass peak. The brush itself barely changes: h50 5.4-5.7 nm
  (n.s.), h95 12.6-14.2 nm (p 5e-4), Rg 5.25-5.75 nm.
- RGD exposure is NOT affected by placement (RGD height 6.2-6.7 nm, p 0.05; fraction above h50 p 0.24). Choosing a placement
  for crosslinking costs nothing in RGD presentation.
- Timing: half of the final bridges form within ~5 ns of crosslinking starting (clustered ~17 ns); the last bonds can appear
  up to ~390 ns later. Event times are not kinetics (caveat from the simulation tool itself).
- Run-level descriptor GLM (bridges/22): log minimum K-K spacing OR 1.9 per e-fold (p<1e-6), number of blocks carrying K
  OR 1.5 per block, K centroid irrelevant; pseudo-R2 0.76, CV R2 0.80. Within random only spacing matters. The class label
  still adds information (p 8e-8): resolved by the per-lysine model below.

B5. The mechanism: per-lysine model (analysis_lysplace/stats/lysine_model.txt)
- P(bridge) rises with the sequence distance from a lysine to the nearest other lysine on the SAME chain: odds x3.43 per
  e-fold [2.98, 3.95], p < 1e-16. A pinned nearest neighbour helps (OR 1.51, it cannot be a loop partner). Number of K within
  30 residues and position along the chain: not significant. P(loop) mirrors this (OR 0.35 per e-fold).
- Reading the curve: nearest neighbour 5 residues away -> ~13 % bridge; ~20 residues -> ~50 %; ~60 -> ~80 %; >100 -> ~90 %.
- Held-out check (10-fold CV by run): expected bridges = sum P(bridge)/2 reproduces the run counts with R2 0.88, RMSE 2.2 bridges;
  class means within 1 bridge.

B6. Optimal design and the definition of "optimal"
- Frozen rule: (1) most bridges; (2) a class counts only if its network is connected in >= 90 % of runs; (3) among ties,
  more distinct chain pairs, then smaller spread. RESULT: no class passes step 2 (best is 48 %). The rule was left
  unchanged and reported as failing (dated note in settings.yaml); a better gate should be defined in advance in a future round.
  On the primary response and all tie-breaks, one-lysine-per-block designs (uniform/scramble) rank first and are
  statistically indistinguishable.
- Design search over all 211,876 placements (pinning averaged): expected bridges rise with the minimum K-K spacing and plateau:
  best achievable 12.2 (min spacing 5 residues), 16.1 (20), 18.4 (40), 19.3 (60), 19.8 (80), 20.0 (100). Top designs
  (e.g. 1R5G10D11, 1R3G8D11, 2R4G9D10; ~20.06 [19.6, 20.5] expected bridges) space the lysines ~100-105 residues apart -- the most
  even spacing the 14-11-15-11 layout allows, inside the data range (one simulated design reaches 105).
- Their advantage over a good uniform design (~19.5) is about half a bridge, smaller than run-to-run SD (2-3): a PLATEAU, not
  a single winner. DESIGN RULE for the wiki: maximise the smallest sequence distance between any two lysines on a chain;
  >= 60 residues gets within ~1 bridge of the optimum; never put two lysines within ~20 residues (loops win). Where along
  the chain, or in which block, does not matter beyond that.

B7. Caveats to state in the wiki
- No replicates: one design = one run, ~+-2-3 bridges of noise. Confirm the top designs with 3 replicates each.
- Crosslinking is first-encounter and irreversible (p = 1); lower reaction probabilities may favour bridges more. The ranking
  should hold, the size of the gaps may not.
- "Connected" ignores the surface. The brush is one density on a wall; transfer to a bulk gel is open.
- The per-lysine model treats lysines as independent given the predictors; valence-1 competition is absorbed in the coefficients.
- The ranking of classes on bridges is not blind (see B3).

B8. Figures (analysis_lysplace/figures/, HTML with hover + PNG)
- class_comparison (6 panels: bridges, loops, pairs bridged, connected cluster, bridge height, bridges above h50),
  z_profiles (density vs height per class: all beads, bridges, loops, RGD), lysine_bridge_probability (bridge/loop probability
  vs distance to nearest lysine, observed bins with CIs plus model), lysine_model_check (held-out predicted vs observed bridges),
  design_rule (bridges vs minimum spacing, model best/median plus simulated runs), bridge_timecourse (bridges vs time per class).
