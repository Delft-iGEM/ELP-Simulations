======================================================================================
0. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY (good for the top of the wiki page)
======================================================================================
We compared 130 simulations of the RGD+ construct (16 chains, 4 lysines per chain, crosslinking ON, 400 ns) that differ
ONLY in where the four lysines (K) sit along the chain. Almost every run forms nearly all of the 22 possible crosslinks, so
the NUMBER of crosslinks says little. What placement decides is WHERE the bonds go: inter-chain bridges (useful, they
build the network) versus intra-chain loops (wasted, they do nothing for the network). Median bridges per run:
clustered 3, focused 7, random 14, uniform 19, scramble 19.5. One variable explains almost all of it: the sequence distance
from a lysine to the NEAREST OTHER LYSINE ON ITS OWN CHAIN. Close neighbours react with each other and form loops; far-apart
lysines reach other chains and form bridges. Design rule: spread the four lysines as far apart as the construct allows
(ideally ~100 residues apart; at least 60 residues gets within ~1 bridge of the best). Placement also changes WHERE in the
brush the network sits, but not the brush height or how exposed the RGD is.

======================================================================================
1. THE QUESTION AND WHY WE DID IT
======================================================================================
- Goal (from Chris): which lysine arrangement (focused, uniform, clustered, random) gives the most INTER-chain crosslinks,
  and how do we define and find an "optimal" arrangement?
- Why inter-chain: a crosslink between two lysines on the SAME chain is a loop (elastically inactive). A crosslink between
  lysines on DIFFERENT chains is a bridge; bridges are what turn a brush of separate chains into a connected network/gel.
- This is a sub-submodule of the lysine work, next to SubM4. In SubM4 crosslinking was OFF and the response was a proxy
  (inter-chain lysine-lysine contacts). Here crosslinking is ON, so we measure the REAL crosslink network instead of a proxy,
  plus the z-height (distance from the wall) structure of the brush and of the bonds.

======================================================================================
2. GLOSSARY (define these on the wiki page)
======================================================================================
- Construct / chain: 357 residues = LQ + 4 x [RGD domain + ELP block + VP]. RGD domain = LDASTVYAVTGRGDSPASSAASA (contains
  the GRGDS motif). ELP block = (VPGXG)n repeats, blocks of 14, 11, 15, 11 repeats; X = I (isoleucine) except the 4 designed
  K and M at repeat 6 of blocks 2 and 4. So there are 4 RGD domains per chain (the "RGD+" part).
- Lysine (K): the crosslinkable residue. 4 per chain, 64 in the 16-chain system.
- Pinned lysine: 19 of the 64 lysines (30 %, "preattached 0.3") are tethered to the surface at t = 0 and stay there. They
  anchor the brush to the wall and cannot react with other lysines. So 45 lysines are free; with valence 1 (each lysine can
  make only ONE bond) the maximum is floor(45 / 2) = 22 crosslinks per run.
- Crosslink: forms when two free K beads come within 0.8 nm; reaction probability p = 1 (first encounter, irreversible),
  valence 1, crosslinking starts at 10 ns.
- Bridge: inter-chain K-K crosslink. n_bridges (at end of run) is the PRIMARY response.
- Loop: intra-chain K-K crosslink (both lysines on the same chain).
- Distinct chain pairs bridged: how many different chain pairs are joined by at least one bridge.
- Secondary loop: an extra bridge between two chains that are already joined (n_bridges - distinct pairs).
- Largest connected cluster: the largest set of chains linked by bridges, as a fraction of 16. "Connected network" = all 16
  chains in one cluster (the surface is NOT counted as a node, so chains linked only via their pins count as separate).
- Cycle rank: n_bridges - 16 + number of components = number of independent cycles in the chain network.
- Unreacted lysines: free lysines left without a partner.
- h50 / h95: brush heights where 50 % / 95 % of the mass lies below (mass-weighted, above the wall).
- Design class (name suffix on the run): see section 3.

3. DATA
======================================================================================
130 runs from five run sheets (all passed QC: completed, 5714 frames, a single job, 19 of 64 lysines pinned at t = 0 and
none during the run, valence 1, box checks pass):
  Chr_RGD+_Lysine_Sweep.csv              40 runs: 10 uniform, 10 focused, 10 clustered, 10 random
  Chr_RGD+_Lysine_sweep_20.csv           20 runs: 7 uniform, 7 focused, 6 random
  Chr_RGD+_Lysine_sweep_30.csv           30 runs: 7 clustered, 8 uniform, 8 focused, 7 random
  Chr_RGD+_lysRandom.csv                 20 runs: random
  Chr_RGD+_lysine_scramble_sweep_20.csv  20 runs: no class suffix -> called "scramble" (one K per block)
Totals per class: clustered 17, focused 25, random 43, uniform 25, scramble 20.

Constant across runs: 16 chains, C = 0.04 chains/nm2, ionic strength 0.19 M, 293.15 K, 400 ns (5714 frames, one per 70 ps).
Only the K positions differ.

Run-name code: positions are K repeat numbers per block, in order block 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 (written R / G / D for blocks 2/3/4
in the names). Example "13R1G13D4" = block 1 repeat 13, block 2 repeat 1, block 3 repeat 13, block 4 repeat 4. "0R0G0D3+4+5+7"
= four K in block 4 (0 = no K in that block). The name was cross-checked against the K positions in each run's simulated
molecules.fasta for all 130 runs (all matched).

The five classes (class and closest-K-K spacing are almost the same variable; only "random" covers the whole range):
  clustered : 4 K in one block, adjacent repeats                          closest K-K spacing 5 residues
  focused   : 4 K in one block, spread out                                5-15 residues
  random    : 19 two-block, 19 three-block, 3 one-per-block, 2 one-block  5-80 residues
  uniform   : one K per block                                             30-105 residues
  scramble  : one K per block (the sheet without a class suffix)          30-105 residues

IMPORTANT: NO DESIGN WAS SIMULATED TWICE. There are no replicates, so noise is judged only from the spread within each class.

======================================================================================
4. HOW THE ANALYSIS WAS DONE (pipeline, in order)
======================================================================================
All scripts are in analysis_lysplace/scripts/, fixed random seed 20261016, run all with scripts/run_all.sh.

Step 0  00_inventory.py   Builds the design table (run_inventory.csv): K positions, design descriptors (minimum K-K spacing,
                          number of blocks carrying K, K centroid), metadata and QC. Reads sheets, molecules.fasta,
                          metadata.csv, crosslink.yaml, surface.yaml. No trajectory frames and no responses.
Step 1  01_extract.py     ONE pass over each DCD trajectory (~12 s per run; extract.sbatch for the cluster, or --workers 8).
                          Stores per run (timeseries/<run>.npz): brush h50/h95, per-frame heights of every crosslink
                          (midpoint z of the two bonded beads), per-lysine height, RGD height (R, G, D beads of the four
                          GRGDS motifs), Rg and end-to-end distance per chain, and 2 A z-profiles. Heights are above the
                          wall (z_wall = 1.9 nm); z is the surface normal. Only runs without an .npz are extracted.
Step 2  02_metrics.py     Network metrics from crosslink_events.csv (bridges, loops, formed, distinct pairs, secondary loops,
                          largest cluster, cycle rank, unreacted, network at 100 ns, bond timing t50/t90) and the per-lysine
                          and per-bond tables. Consistency is asserted: lysine states reproduce the network counts, 19 pinned
                          per run. Outputs runs_metrics.csv, per_lysine.csv (8320 lysines), per_bond.csv (2750 crosslinks),
                          profiles.npz.
Step 3  03_compare.py     Class comparison: Kruskal-Wallis over the 5 classes; pairwise Mann-Whitney U with Holm correction
                          over the 10 pairs; Cliff's delta with bootstrap 95 % CI (10 000 resamples); medians with bootstrap
                          CIs; quasi-binomial GLM (logit) of n_bridges/22 on the design descriptors (all runs and random only,
                          5-fold CV); and the frozen optimality rule.
Step 4  04_lysine_model.py  Per-lysine logistic GEE (see section 6), run-level 10-fold CV, and an exhaustive design search over
                          all 211 876 placements of 4 K on the 49 non-M VPGXG positions.
Step 5  05_figures.py     Plotly figures (HTML with hover + PNG), MOSAIC style; one blue ramp for the ordered classes (light =
                          clustered ... dark = uniform), marker shapes as a second cue for colour-blind readers.

Why statistics were chosen this way (for a "methods" paragraph):
- Rank-based tests (Kruskal-Wallis, Mann-Whitney) + Cliff's delta because bridge counts are small, bounded, and not normal.
- Holm correction because all 10 class pairs are tested.
- A per-lysine model (GEE, exchangeable within run) because the mechanism acts on individual lysines and the 130 runs have
  too few degrees of freedom to see it at run level; GEE accounts for lysines in the same run being correlated.

------------------------------------------------------
Frozen definitions (settings.yaml) and honesty note
------------------------------------------------------
settings.yaml was written BEFORE any network-topology, height, timing or per-lysine quantity was computed. It fixes the
primary response, secondary responses, production window, how heights are measured, the tests, the per-lysine model
predictors, and the optimality rule.
- Production window: frames 1428-5713 (discard first 100 ns, keep 300 ns), same as SubM4.
- Bond height = midpoint z of the two bonded beads, every production frame after the bond formed. Brush h50/h95 mass-weighted.
- RGD height = mean z of the R, G, D beads of the four GRGDS motifs; also fraction above h50 (exposure proxy).
- HONEST DISCLOSURE (keep this wording on the wiki): before freezing, the per-class MEAN bridge counts had been seen once in
  a data check of the event logs (uniform ~18.5, scramble ~18.6, random ~13.4, focused ~7.0, clustered ~3.1). So the class
  RANKING on the primary response is NOT a blind result. Everything else (network topology, z distributions, timing,
  per-lysine outcomes, descriptor effects) was computed after freezing.
- Dated notes at the end of settings.yaml record what changed in interpretation afterwards (the optimality gate, and
  uniform = scramble).

5. RESULTS: COMPARING THE ARRANGEMENTS (stats/summary.txt, figures/class_comparison, z_profiles, bridge_timecourse)
======================================================================================
Medians per class (n = 17 / 25 / 43 / 25 / 20):

                                       clustered focused random uniform scramble
  bridges (PRIMARY)                        3        7      14      19     19.5     (Kruskal-Wallis p = 1e-20)
  loops                                   16       14       8       3      2.5
  crosslinks formed (of max 22)           19       21      22      22      22
  distinct chain pairs bridged             3        7      13      18      18
  largest connected cluster (of 16)      0.13     0.19    0.69    0.94    0.94
  % runs with ALL 16 chains connected      0        0      14      48      45
  cycle rank                               0        0       2       5       5
  bridge height above wall (nm)           1.5      2.2     6.1     7.2     7.3
  fraction of bridges above h50           0.00     0.00    0.55    0.70    0.70
  brush h95 (nm)                         14.2     13.3    13.5    12.6    12.7      (p = 5e-4)
  RGD height (nm)                         6.7      6.4     6.4     6.2     6.2      (p = 0.05)
  Rg (nm)                                5.75     5.56    5.44    5.25    5.26

Key points:
1. CROSSLINKING SATURATES. Random, uniform and scramble almost always form all 22 possible bonds. In clustered designs ~7
   lysines (median) stay unreacted because their partners on the same chain are used up or pinned. So "how many crosslinks"
   is the wrong question; "how many are bridges" is the right one.
2. Every pair of classes differs on bridges (Holm p < 1e-4, Cliff's delta -0.75 to -1.0), EXCEPT uniform vs scramble
   (p = 0.94, delta 0.01 [-0.32, 0.35]). Uniform and scramble differ on none of the 23 responses (all Holm p > 0.6), so the
   scramble sheet is effectively a second uniform set. Report both; pool as "one per block" (48 runs, median 19 bridges)
   where useful.
3. WHERE THE NETWORK SITS (z-structure). In clustered and focused designs every crosslink, bridge or loop, lies within ~3 nm
   of the wall (0 % of bridges above h50): the pinned lysine pulls its whole K block down, so all reactions happen in the
   surface layer. In spread designs bridges peak at 6-7 nm, above the mass peak, ~70 % of them above h50. The brush itself
   barely changes: h50 5.4-5.7 nm (not significant), h95 within 1.6 nm.
4. RGD EXPOSURE is not affected by placement (RGD height p = 0.05, fraction above h50 p = 0.24). Choosing a placement for
   crosslinking costs nothing in RGD presentation. (Wiki: say "no significant effect", not "proven equal".)
5. TIMING. Half of the final bridges form within ~5 ns of crosslinking starting in every class except clustered (~17 ns). The
   last bonds appear up to ~390 ns later, so the network is still finishing in some runs. Event times are descriptive only,
   they are NOT kinetics.
6. RUN-LEVEL DESCRIPTORS (stats/descriptor_glm.csv, quasi-binomial on bridges/22): log minimum K-K spacing, odds ratio 1.9 per
   e-fold (p < 1e-6); number of blocks carrying K, OR 1.5 per block; K centroid irrelevant. Pseudo-R2 0.76, CV R2 0.80.
   Within the random class only spacing matters (number of blocks p = 0.68). The class label still adds information beyond
   these descriptors (p = 8e-8); the per-lysine model (section 6) resolves this: what counts is each lysine's OWN nearest
   neighbour, not the chain's minimum.

------------------------------------------------------
The optimality rule and what happened to it
------------------------------------------------------
Frozen rule (settings.yaml): (1) most bridges; (2) a class/design counts as optimal only if its network is connected (all 16
chains) in >= 90 % of its runs; (3) among ties, more distinct pairs bridged, then smaller between-run spread (IQR).
RESULT: step 2 is met by NO class (best is uniform at 48 %). With 4 lysines per chain and 19 of 64 pinned, one fully
connected network is not reliable for any arrangement at this density. The rule was left unchanged and reported as failing;
this is a finding, not a bug. A future rule should state connectivity as a reported outcome, or use a gate grounded in a
target (e.g. a percolation requirement) set in advance. On the primary response and every tie-break, uniform and scramble
(one lysine per block) come first and are indistinguishable.

======================================================================================
6. THE MECHANISM: THE PER-LYSINE MODEL (stats/lysine_model.txt, figures/lysine_bridge_probability, lysine_model_check)
======================================================================================
Unit: each of the 5850 free (non-pinned) lysines (130 runs x 45). Logistic GEE, exchangeable within run, for P(bridge) and
P(loop). Predictors were fixed in advance: log distance (residues) to the nearest other K on the same chain; number of other
K within 30 residues; whether that nearest K is pinned; relative position along the chain (linear + quadratic).

  P(bridge) predictor                         odds ratio [95 % CI]       p
  log distance to nearest other K on chain    3.43 per e-fold [2.98, 3.95]   < 1e-16
  other K within 30 residues                  0.94 [0.81, 1.09]              0.42
  nearest K is pinned                         1.51 [1.20, 1.90]              5e-4
  position along chain (linear, quadratic)    n.s.                           0.23, 0.41

- P(loop) mirrors it: OR 0.35 per e-fold of distance; 0.41 if the nearest K is pinned.
- Plain-language explanation: lysines that sit close together on a chain find each other first and react (first encounter,
  irreversible, valence 1), forming a loop and using themselves up. Lysines far from any chain-mate are more likely to meet a
  lysine on a different chain. A pinned neighbour helps because a pinned lysine cannot be a loop partner.
- Reading the curve: nearest neighbour 5 residues away -> ~13 % chance of a bridge; ~20 residues -> ~50 %; ~60 -> ~80 %;
  >100 -> ~90 %. ("e-fold" = multiply the distance by e = 2.718.)
- Position along the chain and which block the K is in do NOT matter once the neighbour distance is known.
- Held-out check: expected bridges per run = sum of P(bridge) / 2 over its free lysines; 10-fold CV by run gives R2 = 0.88,
  RMSE 2.2 bridges. Class means reproduced within 1 bridge (clustered 3.06 obs vs 3.67 pred; focused 7.00 vs 6.09; random
  13.42 vs 13.52; uniform 18.48 vs 18.77; scramble 18.55 vs 18.78).

======================================================================================
7. HOW TO OPTIMISE PLACEMENT: THE DESIGN SEARCH (stats/design_*.csv, figures/design_rule)
======================================================================================
- We scored ALL 211 876 ways to put 4 K on the 49 non-M VPGXG positions (C(49,4)), averaging over pinning analytically (each K
  pinned with probability 19/64), expected bridges per 16 chains. Range over all designs: 2.7 to 20.1. 99.5 % of designs lie
  inside the data range of the nearest-neighbour distance (recommendations are only made inside that range).
- Best achievable expected bridges by minimum K-K spacing:

    minimum spacing (residues)     5     20     40     60     80    100
    best expected bridges        12.2   16.1   18.4   19.3   19.8   20.0

  Rises steadily, then levels off.
- Top designs (e.g. 1R5G10D11, 1R4G10D11, 3R5G10D11, 1R3G8D11, 2R4G9D10; ~20.05 [19.6, 20.5] expected bridges) all put the
  lysines ~100-105 residues apart: the most even spacing the 14-11-15-11 block layout allows, and inside the simulated range
  (one simulated design reaches 105). The best design stays in the 19.6-20.5 band in all 200 parameter draws.
- Their predicted advantage over a good uniform design (~19.5) is about HALF a bridge, smaller than the run-to-run spread
  (SD 2-3 within uniform and scramble). So the top is a PLATEAU, not a single winner.
- DESIGN RULE: maximise the smallest sequence distance between any two lysines on a chain.
    * aim for >= 60 residues (within ~1 bridge of the optimum);
    * ideally even spacing, ~100 residues for 4 K on 357 residues (one K per block, spread within the blocks);
    * never place two lysines within ~20 residues of each other (below that, loops win);
    * where along the chain, and in which block, does not matter beyond that.

======================================================================================
8. CAVEATS (state these on the wiki)
======================================================================================
- No replicates: one design = one run, ~+-2-3 bridges of run-to-run noise. Confirm the top designs with 3 replicates each
  before claiming a specific winner.
- The class ranking on the primary response is not blind (section 4).
- The optimality connectivity gate (>= 90 % connected) failed for every class (section 5).
- Crosslinking is first-encounter and irreversible (p = 1). Lower reaction probabilities may favour bridges more, since close
  neighbours would no longer win every race. The ranking should hold; the size of the gaps may not.
- "Connected" ignores the surface: chains linked only through their pins count as separate.
- The per-lysine model treats lysines as independent given the predictors; valence-1 competition is absorbed into the
  coefficients (the run-level check shows it works over this design space).
- Single density (0.04 chains/nm2) on a wall, 0.19 M, 293.15 K. How heights and networks carry over to a bulk gel is open.
- Class and spacing are almost the same variable, which is why the per-lysine model (not the class label) is used for the
  mechanism.
